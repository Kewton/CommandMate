     [38;2;238;238;238mLighthouses have guided sailors through darkness, storms, and dangerous [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mcoastal waters for thousands of years. Their basic purpose is simple: to [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mprovide a visible signal that helps ships determine their location and [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mavoid hazards. Yet the history of lighthouses reflects much more than [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mmaritime safety. It includes the growth of ancient civilizations, the [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mexpansion of trade and naval power, advances in engineering, and the [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mdevelopment of modern technology.[38;2;255;255;255m

     [38;2;238;238;238mThe earliest lighthouse-like structures were probably simple fires lit [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mon hills or towers near harbors. Ancient Mediterranean sailors used such [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238msignals long before formal lighthouses were built. One of the first [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mdocumented lighthouse structures was constructed at Piraeus, the port of [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mAthens, during the fifth century BCE. Greek and Roman authorities [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mrecognized that a permanent elevated light could help ships enter [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mharbors safely, especially at night or during poor visibility.[38;2;255;255;255m

     [38;2;238;238;238mThe most famous lighthouse of the ancient world was the Pharos of [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mAlexandria in Egypt. Construction began around 300 BCE during the reign [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mof Ptolemy I Soter and was completed around 280 BCE under his son, [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mPtolemy II Philadelphus. Designed by the architect Sostrates of Cnidus, [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mthe Pharos stood on the island of Pharos near the entrance to [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mAlexandria’s harbor. Ancient writers described it as having three major [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238msections: a square lower level, an octagonal middle section, and a [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mcylindrical upper tower. At the summit, workers maintained a large fire [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mwhose light may have been magnified by polished metal mirrors.[38;2;255;255;255m

     [38;2;238;238;238mof the tallest structures in the ancient world. Its light guided ships [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mtoward Alexandria, a major center of commerce and learning. The [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mlighthouse survived for many centuries but was gradually damaged by [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mearthquakes. Major earthquakes in 956, 1303, and 1323 CE weakened the [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mstructure, and by 1480 the remaining stones were incorporated into the [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mQaitbay Citadel. The name “pharos” later became a general word for [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mlighthouse in several languages.[38;2;255;255;255m

     [38;2;238;238;238mThe Romans built lighthouses throughout their empire. One of the best-[38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mpreserved examples is the Tower of Hercules near A Coruña, Spain. [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mOriginally constructed during the late first or early second century CE, [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mprobably under the emperor Trajan, it helped ships navigate the [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mnorthwestern coast of the Iberian Peninsula. The Romans also built [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238msignal towers along the English Channel, the Mediterranean, and the [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mAtlantic coast. Their lighthouses supported military fleets, grain [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mshipments, and the movement of goods across the empire.[38;2;255;255;255m

     [38;2;238;238;238mAfter the fall of the Western Roman Empire in the fifth century, many [38;2;255;255;255m
     [38;2;238;238;238mlighthouses fell into disrepair. Coastal trade declined in some regions, [38;2;255;255;255m

     [38;2;238;238;238mtowers. Nevertheless, fires continued to be lit on headlands and near [38;2;255;255;255m

     [38;2;238;238;238mdependable navigation aids increased. In 1611, the first Eddystone [38;2;255;255;255m
